Persian roots, Hebrew words, Caucasian home

Juhuri, also called Judeo-Tat, belongs to the southwestern branch of the Iranian languages. It is close to Tat and related to Persian, yet speakers of Persian cannot understand it.

Hebrew and Aramaic words run through it, and it keeps the Semitic sound ayin, which none of its neighbouring languages has. Its own name, zuhun ʤuhuri, simply means "Jewish language".

Most families have moved to Russian, Azerbaijani, Hebrew or English. Grammars exist, but there are still few recordings of how the language is actually spoken at home. Families are the ones who can change that.

Qırmızı Qəsəbə, Quba

The all-Jewish town across the river from Quba, also known as Krasnaya Sloboda, is the one place where children still learn Juhuri at home. Our first Juhuri evenings will be held here.

Words of our grandparents

Greetings, blessings, family names, numbers and food, collected from open sources where speakers say them aloud. Search, practise, and attach your own family's pronunciation to any word.

Latin spellings follow each source; families and dialects may say a word differently. Words without Cyrillic are shown as the speaker wrote them in English letters. Recordings you attach play only in this browser.

Five sounds to listen for

Cyrillic Juhuri uses letter pairs for sounds that Russian does not have. Hebrew words keep their throat sounds, as among other Mizrahi Jews.

Where AI helps, and what we study

AI does the slow work of writing down and translating. Elders keep the final word. The research asks how far a speech model can go with very little data.

How accurately can modern multilingual speech models recognise Juhuri when only a few hours of transcribed family recordings exist, and how much do elders' corrections improve them?

Measured with word error rate (WER) and character error rate (CER) on speakers the model has never heard.
